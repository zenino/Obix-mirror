Proficiency

Four levels — each with two steps

CL1

Apply

"You do the work the way you are shown."

Applies defined methods to routine work, keeps records, tracks progress against a plan.

CL2

Execute

"You do it on your own — and fix it when it goes wrong."

Works independently, analyses deviations and resolves non-routine issues.

CL3

Lead

"You run many at once and choose what matters most."

Leads delivery across workstreams, manages trade-offs, improves practice.

CL4

Govern

"You decide how everybody should do the work."

Sets standards, governance and direction across projects; manages systemic risk.

A · Developing

Can do the work — but still asks for help with the hardest part.

B · Established

Does the hardest part alone — and finds ways to do it better.

Eight small steps beat four big ones — CL1A → CL4B. People can see progress every year, not once in five years.

How each rung is assessed

Three lenses, applied at every level

Core Knowledge

What must the person know to work at this level?

Core Behaviour

How must they consistently act — problem solving, decisions, accountability, handling conflict?

Specialist Knowledge

What technical capability must they show?

Step 1

Set the level from the nature of the work shown

Step 2

Score the four components → a weighted %

Step 3

Apply that level's B-gate to decide A or B

The level is set by the nature of the work — never by the score.

The rule behind every evidence entry

How Developing (A) evidence differs from Established (B)

A · Developing

Work done in its familiar, assigned form, with support used on the hardest part. Evidence may be assigned, supervised or confirmed by others.

B · Established

The level's defining demand met unaided — plus one improvement the person drove. From CL2 up, evidence must show an outcome.

Three tests when A and B evidence look the same

1

Who started it?

Told to do it, or routine → A. Saw the need and acted → B.

2

Was help used on the hardest part?

Expected at A. At B, it disqualifies.

3

Is there an improvement?

B needs one — a practice changed or a cause removed. This is the most common gap and the main cause of over-grading.

Example · C1 Formwork execution

What each level looks like on site

CL4

Sets the formwork standard and inspection regime, specifies system and vendor needs, and governs panel life and capex.

CL3

Sets the erection method across towers, resolves panel shortfalls, coaches foremen and improves cycle time.

CL2

Runs a floor unaided, diagnoses why a panel won't seat, judges de-shuttering timing, and separates out damaged panels.

CL1

Locates the day's panels on the drawing, erects to sequence, checks line, level and plumb, and reports a misfit instead of forcing it.

Same event, two rungs: an engineer who applied a known fix and escalated the harder call is CL2A. One who diagnosed the causes, chose the fix himself and stopped it recurring is CL2B.
